# Native water bubbles

`BubbleCheck` / `SetupBubble` in the native PRG (source lines 5258–5314) operate three slots, visited 2 → 0 after Mario control and before enemy control. State is X page/low `$83+s/$9c+s`, Y high/low `$cb+s/$e4+s`, fraction `$042c+s`, shared timer `$0792`. `PseudoRandomBitReg+1+s & 1` selects subtracters `$ff/$50` and timer reloads `$40/$20`. Subtraction wraps the Y low byte before comparing with `$20`; zero-initialized slots therefore first move to `$ff`, rather than the hidden marker `$f8`. A slot marked `$f8` waits for the shared timer to expire before spawning at Mario X+9 facing right, X facing left, and native Y+8.

The engine samples Mario after background contacts but before `ProcessWhirlpools`. Its death routine continues moving bubbles while `TimerControl=$ff` counts down; that controller holds the shared bubble timer. Pause holds both. The lives-screen entrance initializes bubbles on engine 7, one row before the remake's logical play phase. The automatic pipe entrance initializes and moves them on the room-entry row.

`Entrance_GameTimerSetup` calls `SetupBubble` directly, without `BubbleCheck` first masking scratch `$07`. The preceding water floor render leaves the high byte of `Palette1_MTiles`, `$91`, in `$07` (`RenderAreaGraphics` loads `MetatileGraphics_High` into it). Indexing the native ROM past the nominal bubble tables reads `$f9` at `Bubble_MForceData+$91`, and `$04` at `BubbleTimerData+$91`. Those are native instruction bytes. They explain the entrance fraction 7 and timer 4; they are global ROM behavior, with no level or replay offsets. The entry test covers all six captured engine-0→7 water entrances, including the halfway-page entrance with a visible initial bubble.

`tools/harness/native-water-rule-events.mjs` extracts 9,791 normal transitions and six entrances. All slot bytes and the shared timer match the pure native model. To recover pre-whirlpool spawn position from a completed native RAM row, it reads the player rectangle, correcting its cached horizontal position to the actual Mario X when a wall zeroes X speed. Recorded-input regressions additionally check runtime ordering without this reconstruction: all of 7-2, and 2-2 through its first independent gameplay discrepancy (Mario X at 4276). A diagnostic full 2-2 replay first differs in a bubble X at 6268, downstream of that known Mario X discrepancy.

The runtime exports `sim.bubbles` with raw native Y, world X, Y high page, and fraction. Rendering remains the animation worker's scope: native `DrawBubble` uses CHR `$74`, palette 2, and requires Mario Y page 1 and an onscreen bubble. No gameplay field is changed by the bubble model.
